TTFields and Quality of Life in Locally Advanced Pancreatic Cancer



  • Citation: JAMA Netw Open - 2026-11-02 - PMID 43024239 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43024239/) - DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41241 (https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41241)

  • Type: Randomised trial

  • Design: open-label phase 3 RCT secondary analysis, N=562, 26 mo

  • What it says: Among 562 patients with baseline EORTC QLQ-C30 data from PANOVA-3 (279 TTFields plus gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel vs 283 chemotherapy alone), global health status remained stable over 26 months in both arms. Adding TTFields prolonged median time to global health deterioration (7.1 vs 5.7 months; HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.61-0.97) and improved pain, pancreatic pain, and most digestive symptom scales from month 2 onward. Indigestion and altered bowel habit were similar between arms.

  • Practice impact: Consider adding TTFields to gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel in unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer to delay quality-of-life deterioration and pain.

  • Caveat: Open-label design and prespecified secondary analysis of quality-of-life endpoints.




Preoperative Exercise During Neoadjuvant Gastroesophageal Cancer Therapy



  • Citation: JAMA Netw Open - 2026-10-01 - PMID 42949746 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42949746/) - DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40136 (https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40136)

  • Type: Randomised trial

  • Design: single-blind 2-arm RCT, N=207 randomized (202 mITT), 3 yr

  • What it says: In 202 patients (101 per group) with gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma undergoing neoadjuvant therapy, non-completion of curative tumor surgery occurred in 10 of 101 (9.9%) exercise patients versus 8 of 101 (7.9%) controls (OR 1.28, 95% CI 0.48-3.38). Treatment discontinuation (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.38-1.72) and postoperative complications (OR 1.16, 95% CI 0.61-2.20) were similar, while 3-year overall survival was 51.0% vs 65.0% (HR 1.58, 95% CI 1.02-2.45).

  • Practice impact: Do not rely on preoperative exercise training to increase rates of curative tumor surgery after neoadjuvant gastroesophageal cancer treatment.

  • Caveat: Single-blind design and lower 3-year survival in the exercise arm warrant cautious interpretation.

Pediatric Short Bowel Syndrome SIBO Consensus Statement



  • Citation: JAMA Netw Open - 2026-11-02 - PMID 42975907 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42975907/) - DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41939 (https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41939)

  • Type: Expert consensus

  • Design: 3-round Delphi consensus, 28-38 respondents, duration not stated

  • What it says: Reached >=75% consensus across 21 statements on diagnosing and managing small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) in children with short bowel syndrome (SBS), including breath testing, clinical criteria, empiric antibiotics, cycling, and diet. Identified metronidazole as preferred first-line therapy, accepted symptom relief to gauge response, and supported long-term antibiotic cycling for recurrent responsive SIBO. Effect sizes and 95% CIs are not applicable to Delphi consensus statements.

  • Practice impact: Use metronidazole first-line and structured antibiotic cycling in recurrent responsive cases when managing pediatric SBS-related SIBO.

  • Caveat: ⚠ numbers need verification (see footer) — Response rates varied across rounds (33, 38, and 28 of 55 invited experts) due to limited pediatric data.




International Diverticulitis Guidelines Systematic Review



  • Citation: BJS Open - 2026-11-03 - PMID 43076449 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43076449/) - DOI 10.1093/bjsopen/zrag201 (https://doi.org/10.1093/bjsopen/zrag201)

  • Type: Systematic review

  • Design: systematic review of guidelines, 4 guidelines, through Sep 2026

  • What it says: Evaluated 4 international acute diverticulitis guidelines (Germany 2021, ESCP 2020, ASCRS 2020, WSES 2020) selected from 148 screened records. All 4 agreed on selective antibiotic omission in uncomplicated diverticulitis, abscess size-based care, and individualized elective surgery, while diverging on classification, ultrasound use, outpatient criteria, abscess size cutoffs, primary anastomosis versus Hartmann procedure, and perforated/secondary prevention strategies. Effect sizes and 95% CIs are not applicable to a guideline comparison.

  • Practice impact: Omit routine antibiotics selectively in uncomplicated diverticulitis while individualizing surgical and outpatient decisions where international guidelines diverge.

  • Caveat: ⚠ numbers need verification (see footer) — Included only 4 guidelines published in 2020-2021 despite searching through September 2026, without quantitative pooled outcome estimates.




High-Resolution Manometry in Altered Foregut Anatomy Scoping Review



  • Citation: Am J Gastroenterol - 2026-11-12 - PMID 43016244 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43016244/) - DOI 10.14309/ajg.0000000000004250 (https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000004250)

  • Type: Study

  • Design: scoping review and interpretation recommendations, 168 articles, duration not stated

  • What it says: Synthesized 168 studies on high-resolution manometry (HRM) in altered or post-surgical foregut anatomy, concluding the Chicago Classification does not apply directly to these patients. Recommends integrating anatomy and focusing on obstructive states such as post-fundoplication outflow obstruction, post-bariatric junctional changes, and treated achalasia with outflow obstruction (TAOO) versus adequately treated achalasia (ATA) or pseudo-achalasia. Effect sizes and 95% CIs are not applicable to a scoping review.

  • Practice impact: Interpret HRM in altered or post-surgical foregut anatomy using anatomic context and outflow obstruction criteria rather than standard Chicago Classification thresholds.

  • Caveat: ⚠ numbers need verification (see footer) — Scoping review format without formal meta-analytic pooling or standardized diagnostic accuracy metrics across heterogeneous surgical cohorts.

Empagliflozin 10 mg in T2DM With MASLD/MASH Meta-Analysis



  • Citation: BMC Gastroenterol - 2026-08-28 - PMID 43069022 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43069022/) - DOI 10.1186/s12876-026-05200-x (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05200-x)

  • Type: Meta-analysis

  • Design: systematic review, meta-analysis, and TSA, 7 RCTs, N=510

  • What it says: In 7 RCTs (n=510) of adults with type 2 diabetes and MASLD/MASH, empagliflozin 10 mg reduced CAP (MD -19.75 dB/m, 95% CI -36.51 to -3.00) and GGT (MD -16.91 IU/L, 95% CI -22.51 to -11.31), with small ALT reduction (MD -3.35 IU/L, 95% CI -6.32 to -0.39) and no significant LSM (MD -0.50 kPa, 95% CI -1.53 to 0.54) or HbA1c change (MD -0.24%, 95% CI -0.72 to 0.24). Body weight fell by MD -4.31 kg (95% CI -5.56 to -3.06) and waist circumference by MD -3.57 cm (95% CI -4.38 to -2.75).

  • Practice impact: no change to current practice - supports it

  • Caveat: Relies on non-invasive steatosis and stiffness surrogates rather than liver histology, with heterogeneous trial comparators and follow-up.




Diagnostic and Prognostic Value of lncRNA H19 in Gastric Cancer



  • Citation: BMC Gastroenterol - 2026-09-19 - PMID 43073789 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43073789/) - DOI 10.1186/s12876-026-05368-2 (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05368-2)

  • Type: Meta-analysis

  • Design: systematic review and meta-analysis, 17 studies, through Jul 2023

  • What it says: Across 9 diagnostic studies, pooled sensitivity was 0.7472 (95% CI 0.6592-0.8188), specificity 0.8169 (95% CI 0.6922-0.8984), and diagnostic odds ratio 12.66 (95% CI 5.42-29.58), with a bivariate AUC of 0.818. Among 11 prognostic studies, 9 reported an association between elevated lncRNA H19 and worse gastric cancer prognosis.

  • Practice impact: no change to current practice - supports it

  • Caveat: ⚠ numbers need verification (see footer) — High risk of bias, substantial between-study heterogeneity, small study count, and total N not stated in abstract.
